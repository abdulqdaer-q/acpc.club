## 2. Vision, mission, values

**Vision.** To cultivate a strong problem-solving and competitive-programming
culture at Aleppo University and enable its teams to achieve sustained excellence
across ICPC competitions and their stages.

**Mission.** To deliver a continuous training and competition system that connects
students, contestants, alumni, and mentors through roadmaps, contests, educational
content, mentoring, and reliable technical and operational support — developing
student capability, preserving knowledge transfer, and strengthening Aleppo
University's representation.

**Core values (8).** Teamwork (team success over individual achievement) ·
Problem solving (complex problems into verifiable steps) · Critical thinking (test
assumptions, justify with evidence) · Competitive spirit (fair competition as a tool
for growth, not an end) · Knowledge transfer (document and pass experience to new
generations) · Volunteerism (serve without conflicting with academic duties) ·
Integrity and fair play (respect contest rules and IP; reject cheating or leaks) ·
Continuous growth (measure, review, update).

**Strategic objectives (6).**
1. Tiered training serving beginner, intermediate, and advanced levels up to team-level competition.
2. Prepare Aleppo University teams for local, national, regional, and world competitions.
3. Build a sustainable network of trainers, mentors, and alumni, and transfer experience between them.
4. Run trial contests and scientific events under reliable technical and operational standards.
5. Produce high-quality content, roadmaps, and problems in language suited to students.
6. Strengthen Aleppo University's image as a community supporting technical excellence.

## 3. Programs (10)

Programs run as one path: outreach → training and practice → competition, mentoring,
and knowledge transfer. Every activity has a plan, a supervisor, and defined
responsibilities scaled to its size.

1. **Weekly training and camps** — tiered sessions for all levels, delivered by trainers who competed.
2. **Orientation sessions** — start-of-semester intros to competitive programming, ICPC, and how to join.
3. **Training roadmaps** — curated topics and problems, with criteria for moving between levels.
4. **Online contests** — periodic, to build individual skill and measure progress.
5. **Onsite trials** — mock contests under official conditions: team of three, one machine.
6. **Mentoring** — experienced contestants follow committed students one-to-one.
7. **Problem setting and judging** — for local, Syrian, and regional contests, respecting confidentiality and copyright.
8. **Educational content** — explanations, lectures, editorials, including a YouTube channel.
9. **Contest volunteering** — organizing, technical, and media teams for the Aleppo University contest and beyond.
10. **Alumni and career sessions** — linking problem-solving skills to the job market and academic paths.

**Activity delivery standards (5).** A clear educational or competitive goal and
defined target group · a named owner, with date, venue, attendance and results
documented · required approvals before booking, announcing, or contacting external
parties · safety, room capacity, and protection of equipment and data · post-activity
evaluation recorded for the next cycle.

**Responsibility principles (4).** General supervision does not override committees'
technical independence · each officer submits a brief plan and periodic follow-up and

with the named officer · the structure is updated when officers change, after
documenting the decision and notifying the university.

### Governance and internal controls (9)

1. **Plans and reports** — annual or semester plan; brief report on activities, attendance, results, challenges.
2. **Meetings and decisions** — periodic management meeting; key decisions documented with an owner and date.
3. **Succession and handover** — orderly handover of files, accounts, equipment, with a transition period.
4. **Resources and finance** — no fees, donations, or contracts in the club's or university's name without written approval.
5. **Safety and facilities** — respect room capacity and lab/electrical/internet rules; name a safety officer for large events.
6. **Conduct and equal opportunity** — no abuse, harassment, discrimination, or exploitation; a confidential complaints channel.
7. **Data and privacy** — collect the minimum student data; define access; no personal information published without consent.
8. **IP and confidentiality** — protect contest problems before release; respect copyright; never share restricted material or accounts.
9. **Name and identity** — use the university's or organizers' name and logo only within approvals and identity policy.

## 5. Membership

Six tiers. Attending activities does not by itself confer membership.

| Tier | Who |
|---|---|
| Participant / Trainee | Benefits from training or contests; membership not required |
| Contestant Member | Proven experience via SCPC or similar, or a good level on platforms like Codeforces; committed to club activities |
| Trainer / Mentor | Delivers sessions or supervises trainees and roadmaps within the scientific plan |
| Volunteer | Organizational, technical, or media contribution to activities and contests |
| Executive Member | Holds a defined responsibility in the structure; subject to accountability and reporting |
| Alumni Advisor | Supports with experience, training, or advice, outside day-to-day management |

**Principles (4).** Equal opportunity based on competence, commitment, and conduct ·
academic study comes first · continued membership may be reviewed on attendance,
achievement, and conduct under clear and fair procedures · no fees or collections
without university approval and approved financial procedures.

**Student journey.** DISCOVER → LEARN → PRACTICE → TEAM UP → COMPETE → MENTOR

**Annual cycle.** Start of semester: orientation, registration, level placement ·
during semester: weekly training, roadmaps, progress tracking · monthly: one online
contest plus a results review · mid-semester: onsite trial, testing prospective teams
· before SCPC: intensive camp, multiple simulations · after season: performance
report, lessons documented, knowledge-transfer plan.

They are the collective result of contestants, trainers, organizers, alumni, and the
university. Figures marked "per club records" must keep that hedge.

## 7. University relationship

**Coordination commitments (6).** Coordinate activities with the relevant university
body and name a point of contact · organize rooms and computer labs under clear
booking schedules with named officers · complete regulatory approvals before campus
events · use the university name and logo per the identity guide and required review ·
coordinate external correspondence when teams compete under the university's name ·
cooperate on feasible operational needs within regulations.

**Value to the university (6).** An organized training program raising skills in
algorithms, logic, and teamwork · professional preparation and representation of
university teams · student cadres and alumni able to support contests and technical
events · archivable scientific content and roadmaps · reports and data for measuring
participation and impact · a positive youth-facing image of the university's technical
strength.

**Suggested KPIs (8).** Reach and participation (registrations, attendance, teams) ·
continuity (roadmap completion, retention between semesters) · scientific progress
(level transitions, assessments, contest performance) · competitive excellence
(qualified teams, medals, placements) · delivery quality (on-schedule activities,
satisfaction, notes addressed) · knowledge transfer (trainers, mentors, alumni
involved; material documented) · volunteering (count, hours, tasks completed) ·
institutional compliance (regularity of plans and reports, asset integrity,
adherence to approvals).

## 8. Open questions — do not invent answers

- **Which year labels the first World Finals season, 2015 or 2016?** The website
  (achievements timeline and photo gallery) says **2016**, Phuket, Thailand. The
  profile document says **2015**. Both may describe the same event: ICPC names a
  season for its regional year while the finals run the following calendar year, and
  the Phuket finals were held in 2016. The record is already inconsistent — the Dhaka
  finals are labelled **2021** although the club's own photo of that event is banded
  "6–11 November 2022". Pick one convention and apply it to all five seasons. In the
  meantime, avoid naming that year in public copy.
- **The profile document and its published artifact still carry 131 teams / ≈98%.**
  The website carries 135 / ≈105%. The documents need reconciling.
- **Does `hello@aleppo.icpc.club` receive mail?** Unconfirmed, and it is advertised in
  four places on the website.
- **`icpc.club` as a host for other universities' clubs.** The club owns the apex and
  a landing page invites other clubs. Worth confirming with the ICPC regional contact
  first — a multi-club host implies more authority than a single club's site, and
  hosting others' subdomains inherits some responsibility for their content.

## 9. House style for club documents

- **Bilingual, Arabic-primary.** Section titles pair Arabic then English
  (`التعريف بالنادي   CLUB OVERVIEW`); sub-headings use a pipe (`الرؤية  |  VISION`).
- Key statements appear in Arabic followed by an English rendering — a **parallel
  statement, not a literal translation**.
- Values, KPIs, and journey stages are bilingual label pairs
  (`العمل الجماعي  |  TEAMWORK`).
- Tables for roles, membership tiers, milestones, and the annual cycle. Bullet lists
  for principles and standards.
- **Hedge unverified figures** with "per club records / وفق سجلات النادي".
- **Hedge ownership of achievements** — always collective, never the club alone.
- **Always include the affiliation disclaimer** in official and university-facing
  documents.
- **Note title substitutions explicitly** when reproducing the org chart.
- Tone: institutional, restrained, compliance-aware. Commitments phrased as
  obligations ("no fees … without written approval"), not aspirations.
- Close on expanding impact, raising team level, and a clear student path from first
  exposure through competing to mentoring the next generation.
